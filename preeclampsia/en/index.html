Chapter 01 · Physiology

Haemodynamic adaptation – and the concept of unmasking

A normal pregnancy is a months-long cardiovascular stress test. Those who pass it have reserve. Those who fail it most likely never had it.

Course across pregnancy

Normal pregnancy
Week40
  1. 1Step 1 of 4

    A massive volume load

    In a normal pregnancy, plasma volume rises by up to 50% and cardiac output by 30–50%. This preload increase is the physiological basis of haemodynamic adaptation.

  2. 2Step 2 of 4

    Resistance has to fall

    Systemic vascular resistance (total peripheral resistance) falls at the beginning of pregnancy and reaches its nadir in the second trimester (around weeks 14 to 24). Thereafter it rises again towards the third trimester and delivery, returning to pre-pregnancy values.

  3. 3Step 3 of 4

    Blood pressure stays low

    Despite a markedly higher output, mean arterial pressure even falls slightly in the second trimester. MAP = CO × SVR only balances because both factors move in opposite directions.

  4. 4Step 4 of 4

    Intact cardiovascular reserve

    Thanks to intact myocardial and endothelial reserve, the sharp rise in cardiac output in a physiological pregnancy is offset by the fall in peripheral vascular resistance, so that blood pressure remains stable until the end.

Vasodilation · tight barrier · thromboresistant

The vasodilators of pregnancy

  • NONitric oxide

    Produced by the endothelium, diffuses into smooth muscle and lowers tone via cGMP.

  • PGI₂Prostacyclin

    Vasodilatory, inhibits platelet aggregation – its balance with thromboxane tips in preeclampsia.

  • RLXRelaxin

    Increases arterial compliance and renal perfusion early in pregnancy.

Key point

The kink in the curve

Systemic vascular resistance (SVR) stays low until term. Preload and afterload remain matched to one another.

Chapter 02 · Molecular pathogenesis

The anti-angiogenic shift – the trigger sits in the placenta

The cause of preeclampsia lies in the placenta, its consequences appear in the maternal vasculature. Between the two sits a soluble receptor.

The clinical biomarker: sFlt-1/PlGF ratio

12

Angiogenic balance

In clinical practice the anti-angiogenic imbalance is quantified in maternal serum and serves as a highly accurate predictive marker of endothelial failure. Internationally established cut-off values apply (based on the PROGNOSIS study):

  • Rule-out (ratio < 38)Angiogenic balance: A value below 38 rules out the occurrence of preeclampsia requiring treatment for the next seven days with nearly 100% certainty (very high negative predictive value).

  • Grey zone (ratio 38–85 or 38–110): An intermediate risk that requires close clinical monitoring.

  • Rule-in (ratio > 85 in early PE <34 weeks / ratio > 110 in late PE ≥34 weeks): This range is highly predictive of established or rapidly evolving preeclampsia and signals imminent endothelial decompensation, which usually requires timely delivery.

  1. 1Step 1 of 4

    Trophoblast invasion and remodeling

    Extravillous trophoblast migrates into the uterine spiral arteries and replaces endothelium and smooth muscle.

    The spiral arteries transform from narrow, muscular vessels that respond to vasoconstrictor stimuli into wide, rigid, non-reactive conductance vessels. A low-resistance / high-capacitance system emerges.

  2. 2Step 2 of 4

    Metabolic stability

    Vessel remodeling produces optimal oxygenation and metabolic stability. The wide, vasomotor-inactive artery perfuses continuously and with low resistance. The steady, non-pulsatile blood flow protects the syncytiotrophoblast from oxidative stress.

  3. 3Step 3 of 4

    Intact VEGF and PlGF signalling

    The optimally perfused placenta releases pro-angiogenic factors into the maternal circulation: VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) and PlGF (placental growth factor).

  4. 4Step 4 of 4

    Intact signalling

    Because sFlt-1 and sEng levels remain physiologically low, VEGF and PlGF bind unimpeded to the maternal endothelium. This preserves an intact TGF-β cascade, maintains endothelial function and guarantees vasodilation through NO production.

An analogy from oncology

Preeclampsia as an endogenous tyrosine kinase inhibitor

The mechanism mirrors that of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) that block the VEGF axis in oncology. Their characteristic side effects (severe hypertension, proteinuria and heart failure) are the very same organ manifestations seen in preeclampsia. In effect, the placenta administers an endogenous anti-VEGF therapy.

Chapter 03 · Systemic effect

Systemic endothelial dysfunction

At roughly 7,000 m² the endothelium is the body's largest endocrine organ. Remove its VEGF survival signal and it loses tone control, barrier function and thromboresistance all at once.

SVR

Vasoconstriction raises systemic vascular resistance. Blood pressure rises. Not because the heart pumps more, but because the vascular bed closes down.

  1. 1Step 1 of 4

    The maintenance signal is intact

    VEGF and PlGF act as permanent maintenance signals that continuously secure the structural and functional integrity of differentiated endothelium and protect it from apoptosis. This applies especially to the highly fenestrated vascular beds of kidney, liver and brain.

  2. 2Step 2 of 4

    eNOS is upregulated

    Driven by the strong VEGF (and relaxin) signal, endothelial NO synthase activity rises massively. Large amounts of NO diffuse into vascular smooth muscle, maintaining a strong and lasting cGMP-mediated relaxation stimulus.

  3. 3Step 3 of 4

    The ROS–NO balance remains stable

    The formation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) is effectively controlled by cellular antioxidants, so that NO is not neutralised and retains high bioavailability. This intact balance produces the profound vasodilation typical of pregnancy.

  4. 4Step 4 of 4

    Three physiological adaptations

    A healthy endothelial layer yields three essential cardiovascular functions for pregnancy: a drastically reduced afterload, an intact vascular barrier (which prevents pathological oedema and proteinuria), and an undisturbed antithrombotic microcirculation.

  • Afterload ↑

    SVR

    Vasoconstriction raises systemic vascular resistance. Blood pressure rises. Not because the heart pumps more, but because the vascular bed closes down.

  • Capillary permeability ↑

    Oedema · proteinuria

    Interendothelial junctions open and fluid escapes into the interstitium. In the kidney, glomerular endotheliosis destroys the filtration barrier. Classic proteinuria ensues.

  • Procoagulant state

    Platelets ↓

    The activated endothelium loses its thromboresistance: platelet activation, consumption and microangiopathic haemolysis, which leads to HELLP syndrome.

Chapter 04 · Cardiology

Haemodynamic unmasking: failure of ventriculo-arterial coupling

Here the circle closes. A healthy heart handles volume load alone with eccentric remodeling. When the same volume load abruptly meets a steeply increased afterload, ventricle and artery uncouple.

Eccentric (physiological)

Cavity slightly enlarged, wall thickness nearly unchanged, compliance preserved.

  1. 1Step 1 of 5

    Physiological: eccentric remodeling

    In healthy pregnancy the volume load produces mild eccentric hypertrophy – the ventricle grows slightly and stays compliant. This is physiological, reversible remodeling.

  2. 2Step 2 of 5

    Coupling remains optimal

    In a healthy pregnancy the volume load meets an adaptively lowered arterial elastance (Ea). Ventricle and vasculature adjust in synchrony and continue to operate at the energetically optimal ratio.

  3. 3Step 3 of 5

    Adaptive: eccentric remodeling

    The heart responds with a proportional enlargement of the cavity and only mild wall thickening. Wall stress remains balanced. Elastic distensibility (compliance) is preserved in the long term.

  4. 4Step 4 of 5

    Intact diastolic function

    The elastic ventricle takes up the additional volume at unchanged low filling pressures. The heart’s diastolic relaxation capacity remains fully preserved.

  5. 5Step 5 of 5

    What echocardiography shows

    Preserved global longitudinal strain (GLS), a physiologically low E/e' ratio and a normal-sized left atrium (LAVI) as the expression of optimal myocardial and diastolic adaptation.

Pressure-volume relationship

Ees describes ventricular contractility, Ea the load imposed by the arterial system. If Ea rises disproportionately the loop shifts upwards – with falling stroke volume and rising filling pressure.

Key point

How to read the diagram

Each loop corresponds to one cardiac cycle: diastolic filling at the bottom right, the vertical limbs mark isovolumetric contraction and relaxation, the upper segment the ejection phase. The blue line (Ees, end-systolic elastance) quantifies load-independent contractility, the orange line (Ea, arterial elastance) the effective arterial afterload, and the grey curve the end-diastolic pressure-volume relationship (EDPVR) as a measure of passive ventricular compliance. In healthy pregnancy peripheral vasodilation lowers Ea. The Ea/Ees ratio stays well below the clinical cut-off of 0.8. Stroke volume and stroke work remain maximally efficient.

Chapter 05 · Long-term perspective

The dual-hit model: why the risk persists after delivery

Women after preeclampsia carry a 2- to 4-fold increased risk of chronic hypertension, coronary artery disease, heart failure and stroke. Current literature explains this with a dual-hit model.

  1. 1

    Pre-conception

    Latent vulnerability

    Subclinically reduced endothelial and myocardial reserve – clinically silent and not measurable in everyday life.

  2. 2

    Pregnancy

    The stress test

    Volume load, anti-angiogenic shift, rising afterload. The system decompensates – or it does not.

  3. 3

    Postpartum

    Apparent recovery

    sFlt-1 falls rapidly once the placenta is delivered and blood pressure usually normalises. Concentric remodeling and diastolic dysfunction, however, persist in a relevant proportion of women.

  4. 4

    Years to decades

    Manifest disease

    Chronic hypertension, HFpEF, coronary artery disease and cerebrovascular events occur earlier than in the comparison population.

Pillar 1

Shared risk factors – the unmasking

Preeclampsia and cardiovascular disease share the same underlying risk factors. Pregnancy is merely the first stress test the patient fails – the predisposition was already there.

  • Insulin resistance
  • Subclinical inflammation
  • Dyslipidaemia
  • Microangiopathy
Pillar 2

Vascular scarring – the direct damage

Months of anti-angiogenic and hypertensive storm leave irreversible epigenetic and structural damage in endothelium and myocardium: vascular scarring and accelerated vascular ageing.

  • Epigenetic imprinting
  • Endothelial scarring
  • Persistent cardiac remodeling
  • Arterial stiffening

Relative risk after preeclampsia

Orders of magnitude from meta-analyses; the earlier and more severe the preeclampsia, the higher the risk.

  • Chronic hypertension3.7×
  • Heart failure4.2×
  • Coronary artery disease2.2×
  • Stroke1.8×

What this means in practice

A history of preeclampsia is not a closed obstetric episode but a female cardiovascular risk marker. It belongs in every risk stratification – with structured blood pressure, metabolic and, where indicated, echocardiographic follow-up.

Glossary

sFlt-1
Soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1: the soluble ectodomain of VEGF receptor 1, which scavenges circulating VEGF and PlGF.
sEng
Soluble endoglin: a soluble TGF-β co-receptor that amplifies endothelial dysfunction synergistically with sFlt-1.
VEGF
Vascular endothelial growth factor: survival and maintenance signal for differentiated, particularly fenestrated endothelium.
PlGF
Placental growth factor: a placental member of the VEGF family, typically reduced in preeclampsia.
eNOS
Endothelial NO synthase: produces nitric oxide and is the endothelium's central vasodilation switch.
SVR
Systemic vascular resistance: corresponds to left ventricular afterload.
GLS
Global longitudinal strain: a measure of longitudinal myocardial shortening, more sensitive than ejection fraction.
E/e'
Ratio of early mitral inflow velocity to mitral annular tissue Doppler velocity – a surrogate of left ventricular filling pressure.
LAVI
Left atrial volume index: left atrial volume indexed to body surface area, a marker of chronic diastolic burden.
HFpEF
Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction: a typical late consequence of diastolic dysfunction.
Ees
End-systolic elastance: a load-independent measure of ventricular contractility.
Ea
Arterial elastance: a measure of the effective arterial load opposing the ventricle.
